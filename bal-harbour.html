Bal Harbour, FL

Discreet, by design.

Bal Harbour is small, quiet, and easy to overstate. Onyx keeps arrivals the same way: on time, unmarked, and without a fuss at the entrance.

Who this serves

Bal Harbour runs about ten blocks of Collins Avenue, anchored by Bal Harbour Shops and a run of oceanfront residences and hotels. Most bookings here are shopping trips, hotel arrivals, and residence pickups where a marked car or a driver asking for directions is exactly what a client is trying to avoid.

The geography

Collins Avenue is the only through street. Bal Harbour Shops sits at 9700 Collins Avenue; north of the village, Collins continues into Haulover Park and then Sunny Isles Beach, while south it becomes Surfside's Harding and Collins corridor before reaching Miami Beach. The mainland connection is the Broad Causeway (NE 123rd Street on the Miami side), a smaller, quieter crossing than the causeways serving Miami Beach further south.

Airport access

Miami International (MIA) and Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood (FLL) are both realistic options from Bal Harbour, reached by crossing the Broad Causeway to the mainland and then taking I-95. President Donald J. Trump International Airport (DJT, formerly PBI) is a longer trip north, generally only for a specific nonstop.

Trip types

Bal Harbour Shops arrivals and departures are scheduled around specific entrances, not a general drop-off zone. Hotel and residence pickups are typically short, local trips down to Miami Beach or up to Sunny Isles and Aventura for dinner, shopping, or an event.

A note on the village limits

Bal Harbour is a small, incorporated village. Haulover Park, immediately north, is not part of the village — it's a Miami-Dade County park sitting between Bal Harbour and Sunny Isles Beach. Surfside starts immediately to the south along the same stretch of Collins Avenue. The three areas run together on foot, but they are separate jurisdictions, which matters mainly for how an address is written on a booking.

Getting around Bal Harbour

  • Collins Avenue. The single through street, running from Haulover Park in the north to Surfside and Miami Beach in the south.
  • Broad Causeway. The direct mainland connection, smaller and generally quieter than the Julia Tuttle or MacArthur Causeways serving Miami Beach.
  • Bal Harbour Shops entrances. Multiple entrances serve the property — confirming which one at booking avoids a slow loop through the parking structure.
  • Neighboring Surfside. Immediately south along Collins and Harding Avenues — a common add-on stop for residence or hotel pickups.
  • Haulover Park traffic. Weekend beach traffic at this Miami-Dade County park, just north of the village line, can spill onto Collins Avenue and affect timing for pickups near that end of Bal Harbour.

Unmarked, on schedule

The same standard for a Bal Harbour Shops arrival as for a residence pickup later that day — an approved chauffeur and vehicle confirmed for the booking, not an unfamiliar face at the entrance. Seating for up to six passengers covers a shopping trip with bags or a full car heading to dinner.
